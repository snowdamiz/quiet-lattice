F1 — P2: A sleeping destination replays shield-regeneration time

Location: packages/server/src/combat/zone.ts:291 (new transfer clock assignment), :508-514 (new regeneration integration); packages/server/src/zones/manager.ts:209-230 (only newly created zones receive the current clock).

ZoneManager.open wakes a retained sleeping zone without advancing its old zone.time. transfer then replaces the incoming pool's regeneration cursor with that old time. The next step integrates an interval already credited in the source. A new player damaged before the first wake tick also records the old time as hitAt and can skip the six-second delay entirely.

Concrete reproduction: real ZoneManager with its default 30-second sleep policy, injected monotonic/world time, an empty deep:0 opened at t=1000 and slept at t=1031; a player takes 50 shield damage in sys:0, regenerates normally for seven seconds, submits accepted movement across the 6e9 boundary, and transfers using ZoneManager.follow into the sleeping deep:0. Shield immediately before/after transfer is 55. The next 0.05-second tick raises it to 60.25: +5.25 instead of +0.25. A second case joins the sleeping zone at t=1031, takes a fresh 40-point hit, and is back at full 100 shield at t=1031.05 instead of remaining at 60.

Evidence: shield-review.test.ts, shield-sleep.log; both assertions fail on the real manager/movement/zone/combat path. No timer, network, DB or service startup. Fix direction: use the actual handoff/join clock and preserve monotonic elapsed accounting; waking a retained zone must initialize its clock/frame state consistently before damage or placement. Do not rewind the regeneration cursor to the destination's last tick.

F2 — P2: Retiring a cast callback strands the burn status and suppresses a valid new Sear

Location: packages/server/src/abilities/service.ts:419-422. Existing consumers: abilities/thermal.ts:20-35; abilities/statuses.ts:39-47.

The new after wrapper discards the timer when live() becomes false. That prevents ignite's timer from executing its status.remove cleanup. Statuses.apply assumes that a retained status still has its timer and returns false while its one-second end grace is held. Consequently a fresh Sear just after its eight-second cooldown extends the orphan status but creates no new damage loop.

Concrete reproduction: cast shipped Sear on a real creature; its first half-second tick removes 2.5 HP. Enter and leave an event phase using the real PhaseManager, then wait until the original eight-second cooldown ends. Cast Sear again on the same target and advance one second. Both casts report cast, total heat paid is 40, the new readyAt is 1016, but total damage remains 2.5 HP. This fails the actual health assertion, not an attack/state-only assertion. Repeated on-cooldown casts can continue extending the orphan.

Evidence: thermal-review.test.ts, reproductions.log. Fix direction: context retirement needs generation-safe status/timer cleanup, or an explicit effect cancellation path. A fresh application must not reuse a status whose producer was retired. Do not restore late damage merely to let cleanup run.

F3 — P2: A retired Lance still reports on and consumes the next activation as off

Location: packages/server/src/abilities/service.ts:419-422 and :330-343; mismatched toggle authority at :588-605.

The context now retires permanently on presence/life changes, but holds/settle/state do not consult that retired context. Thus a phase departure/return stops the shipped Lance callback forever while its toggle remains registered and replicated as active. The next legitimate cast hits the old-toggle branch and returns ended rather than starting a new beam.

Concrete reproduction: shipped Lance removes 3 HP in its first 0.25 seconds. Enter/leave an event phase, then fire for another 0.5 seconds: damage remains 3, abilities.on is still ["lance"], and the next activation returns result="ended". The target and caster remain alive, in range, on foot and with Lance owned/slotted. This is an existing action consumer regression from the generic context change.

Evidence: thermal-review.test.ts, reproductions.log. Fix direction: include the same context generation validity in toggle settling, call the effect's end exactly once, remove the toggle, and notify clients when the generation retires. Keep retirement and replicated toggle authority consistent.

F4 — P2: Refund/rebuy between ticks preserves old Bond and Regrow work

Location: packages/server/src/abilities/service.ts:342 and :303-305; Bond eligibility closure at :448. Gateway subscription at gateway/ws.ts:411 already calls skillsChanged synchronously.

The live latch only observes current bought state when a timer/AI callback next runs. skillsChanged settles toggles only; neither the non-toggle contexts nor the companion are invalidated at ownership loss. Refund and repurchase before that callback therefore conceal the invalid interval and preserve the old action, contrary to the stated dismissal/no-resurrection contract. Big Game's codex eligibility closure similarly has no generation for a lost-and-restored requirement.

Concrete reproduction through actual SkillService, InventoryService, CodexService, CastService, MeterService, Zone and fauna, with the normal skills.subscribe -> casts.skillsChanged wiring: cast Bond, respec Bond, buy it again before a fauna tick. Both skill operations succeed, the refund charges 500 units (2500 -> 2000), but companion owner remains 2 after the tick. With Regrow, cast at 40 suit HP, successfully refund and rebuy Regrow before its first timer, then run 6.1 seconds: the old cast heals to 80 even though the refund also removed Regrow from the bar. No fresh cast was made.

Evidence: real-skills-review.test.ts, real-skills.log; two failed assertions. Initial mutable-skill-reader probes in symbiotic-review.test.ts/reproductions.log are retained, but the finding relies on the later real service reproduction. The fixture supplies trainerAtHand=true, the intentional trainer authority seam; this proves the public respec/consumer contract, not an independently exercised live trainer UI. Production's default trainer hook is currently false. Fix direction: observe and latch loss of each effect's required ownership/eligibility in the synchronous skill notification; retire the affected context/companion without waiting for the next timer. Unrelated skill changes should not cancel valid work.
