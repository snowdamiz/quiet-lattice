NOTES

- **Design language — 4:** Broad scalloped shell, radiating ribs, overlapping plates, pearl/sea-green palette and spiral fittings clearly express Thalune construction.
- **Silhouette/read — 4:** The broad, low carrier shape remains recognizable at 100 m and 200 m LOD2. Its substantial footprint distinguishes Mule from the narrower comparison hulls.
- **Proportion/scale — 4:** The standing and seated bipeds make the cockpit, ladder and overall hull scale plausible. The seated reference fits between the side consoles. The lineup caption records 19.0 m length, within the specified band.
- **Materials — 3:** Cockpit frames, ladder rails and hardpoint trim show broad, streaky pale wear; the damaged canopy has a conspicuously uniform crack network. **Action:** repaint localized wear around grips, rungs, joints and exposed edges, and give canopy fractures identifiable impact origins with varied crack density.
- **Surface detail — 3:** Overlapping shell geometry, maintenance plates and exposed damage framing are present. The cockpit’s broad blank front fascia and minimally differentiated console blocks remain below the exterior’s finish. **Action:** resolve the main instrument panel with defined bezels, recessed instruments, control groupings and readable markings at seated viewing distance.
- **Topology/LODs — 2:** Across all three lighting cases, LOD2 introduces strong dark/gold bands and warped highlights across panels that look smooth at LOD0. Damaged panels lose their curled forms and become substantially larger dark openings; husked gaps and exposed framing also change markedly. Wireframes show the corresponding coarse panel triangulation. **Action:** rebuild the lower-LOD panel surfaces and preserve their normals, UVs, overlap edges and damage openings before verifying transitions at the intended distances.
- **Consistency — 4:** Mule shares the lineup’s dark glazing, framed panels and mechanical accents while retaining its own shell language. All three liveries are visually distinguishable. Damage and husk remain recognizably Mule.
- **Fitness — 4:** The stills show a boarding ladder, an open canopy, a fitted seat, landing supports and two outward-facing hardpoint mounts. Overlays locate four bays, four gear pivots, two hardpoints and three colliders. This supports static placement, with operational limits below.
- **No placeholder tell — 4:** The layered shell, fitted cockpit enclosure, mount hardware and modeled panel-loss states read as a developed asset. The unfinished console treatment is addressed under surface detail.
- **Performance — 2:** Recorded geometry and per-variant texture allocations meet the stated budgets: LOD0 **141,922 / 148,688 / 138,478** triangles; LOD3 **4.55% / 4.86% / 4.72%**; exported texture allocations **277.3 / 293.3 / 352.0 MB** for base/damaged/husked. However, whole-scene settling times are **69.5/112.1 ms**, **32.2/44.8 ms**, and **30.4/48.0 ms** for noon/night respectively. Every noon/night scene is over budget. Base noon is also unstable; base noon/night and both other night paired estimates are unreliable. Open-space settling is under budget, but husked open-space GPU P95 is **20.935 ms**. **Action:** diagnose the whole-scene bottleneck and supply stable, device-identified measurements meeting the 16.67 ms target across all three cases; establish asset attribution separately.
