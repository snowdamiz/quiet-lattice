## 1. The demo today

What exists today is a demo: it proves the engine, the scale and the feel. This section records it so the rest of the document can say what changes. Nothing in it is owed anything by the design.

**Engine and scope.** three.js + Vite, browser, single player, no backend. Saves in localStorage, one slot. Models generated by Python scripts in headless Blender (ship, multitool, 78 flora meshes, creatures, structures). Procedural audio.

**Universe.** One galaxy ("Nullius Spiral"), a cluster of 70 systems within ~66 ly of the home system (seed 424242). Star classes G/K/M/F/B/E. Each system: 5–7 planets, moons, an asteroid belt, one "Waystation" orbiting the home world. The whole thing is flyable end to end at true scale; warp cells allow instant jumps. Beyond the cluster: 46 other galaxies, 14 nebulae, and "The Edge" (end of the universe, "Expansion 0.0000 c") on the +X side.

**Planets.** 8 archetypes (lush, exotic, desert, frozen, toxic, volcanic, barren, gas giant) + 5 hostile variants (dead, irradiated, inferno, storm, corrosive, crushing). Six ship hazards (heat, cryo, pressure, acid, radiation, storm) with altitude ceilings and damage rates. Weather, day/night, oceans, lava seas. **Cities are planned and the ground is levelled for them, but nothing renders them yet** (layoutCity/streetPlan unused).

**Points of interest.** 110–160 per planet: Abandoned Outpost (units), Ancient Monolith (units, maybe a warp cell, one of 5 lore lines), Crashed Starship (resources), Signal Beacon (reveals all sites). One-shot.

**Fauna.** 1–4 species per living world; quadruped / biped / flyer body plans; herds; idle / wander / flee. Analyze for units. Nothing is hostile.

**Resources & economy.** 9 resources (carbon, ferrite, sodium, oxygen, di-hydrogen, copper, cobalt, tritium, gold), flat prices, sell-only trade. Shop: 3 consumables + 6 one-time upgrades. Start: 2500 units, 1 warp cell.

**Player.** First-person walker with jetpack, sprint, swim. Life support (oxygen refills), hazard protection (sodium refills), health. Death = "suit failure", recall to ship.

**Ship.** Arcade flight, boost, pulse drive (fuel), auto land/take off, docking autopilot, twin lasers (asteroids, pirates), shield + hull, hazard resists. Loss = rebuilt at station for 20% of units.

**Combat.** Random pirate raids in open space (2–3 recoloured player ships, simple pursuit AI). Defeat = towed home for 15% of units.

**Station.** Hangar with 4 pads, trade deck with 2 terminals, a non-interactive hologram trader, 5 NPC traffic ships.

**Progression.** A 7-step tutorial objective chain ending in "THE STARS ARE YOURS". No XP, levels, skills, quests, or factions.

**Ready to build on.** Deterministic world from seeds (ideal for client/server), frames of reference for huge scales, in-engine camera glides/dips/banners (cutscene tooling), scanner pulse + mining beam + ship lasers (first abilities), heat/pulse-fuel meters (ability resources), monolith lore + The Edge (story seeds), planned cities (hubs), hologram trader (first NPC), crash sites (ship-replacement quest).

## 2. Decisions

1. **Classless.** One character type for everyone. Skills are learned along the way as they unlock; every build is unique.
2. **PvP everywhere** except safe zones: a radius around each capital city and a sphere around every Waystation.
3. **Three home planets**, each a civilised world with a large capital city. The player's model is that world's people (the city NPC model). Three playable peoples.
4. **Keep the 70 systems.**
5. **Death.** The character is never lost. Just before death, a Recall animation teleports them to their home planet. The ship is lost and must be bought again, or earned through a home-planet quest if the player has no money.
6. **No real-world coding.** This is a fantasy world. The three peoples are alien species defined by biology and the world that shaped them, never by dress, wealth, work or any real-world culture or region. Every faction draws from all three peoples. No people is the villain; the only villainy is the machine and the small circle that feeds it.
7. **Population-proof, online only.** One shared world, no offline fallback. Shared systems read the lean of whoever is playing, never the volume; NPCs carry every baseline; events and bosses scale to presence; persistent objectives accumulate progress over time instead of needing a simultaneous crowd; hubs and starter regions phase so thousands fit. Where a mechanic cannot pass both tests it is redesigned. See 16.6.
8. **Blender, one high bar, and the demo is disposable.** Every model is hand-authored in Blender to the quality bar in section 19; scripts export and bake, they do not model. Anything in the demo that does not fit the design is reworked or removed (section 20); nothing is kept for sentiment or as a fallback.

## 3. Design pillars

1. One continuous universe, no loading screens, shared with other players.
2. You are a *claimant*, not a soldier: power comes from skills learned from the monoliths, not from guns.
3. Distance is danger: the further from the home worlds and the nearer The Edge, the higher the tier.
4. Discovery is content: first finds are named by players and remembered by the server.
5. The sandbox stays: mine, fly, trade, explore at your own pace; the story is there when you want it.
6. One online world that works the same at five players and five thousand: no mechanic needs a crowd, and none breaks under one.
7. One art bar: everything is hand-made in Blender and reviewed in-engine. Nothing ships as a placeholder.

## 4. Backstory (what happened before the player arrives)

**The Cartographers.** Long before any living people, a civilisation charted this arm of the galaxy. They believed no one could own a star, so they built nothing permanent and left only markers: the monoliths, one on every world, all linked into a single network. Their creed survives as the saying every pilot knows: *the stars belong to no one.*

**The Edge.** The Cartographers discovered the universe had stopped expanding and had begun to fall back on itself from the outside in. Where space has already ended there is a boundary: The Edge, seen from the cluster as a pale opalescent wall on one side of the sky. It moves inward. The systems beyond the cluster on that side are already gone.

**The Count.** The Cartographers turned the monolith network into a machine that holds a pocket of space open around the cluster, so The Edge slows to a crawl here. The machine runs on recorded minds. Every traveller who touches a monolith is copied into it; the copies are what the Count burns. The voice counting backwards is the machine's remaining charge. Some monoliths take more than a copy: they take the whole pilot. That is why crashed ships have no pilot in them.

**The Patient.** The Cartographers did not stay to tend the machine. They went to sleep beneath a handful of worlds, to wait for the Count to run out. When it does, their plan is to collapse what remains of the cluster into a single point of light and push it through The Edge as the seed of a new universe. Everyone recorded in the Count goes with it. Everyone still living does not. They are not villains; they are the only people who ever had a plan for the end. The plan simply has no place for anyone alive now.

**The Hollow.** What is left when a monolith takes a pilot whole: ships and bodies with nothing inside, still flying old courses. They drift inward from the Edge-ward systems and attack anything that still has a mind in it, because the network draws them toward the uncopied.

**The peoples.** Three peoples settled the cluster long after, each shaped by its own world: the amphibious Thalune of the tidal coasts, the crystal-symbiont Ixari of the spire forests, the fungal Ulgani of the spore moors. Each raised its capital around a monolith without knowing what it was, and each of those three stones is an anchor of the Count. The Waystation Compact grew out of the first shipyards on all three worlds and strung stations between the stars. People touched the monoliths and learned the disciplines from the memories inside: that is where every skill in the game comes from. Nobody connected the missing pilots to the stones.

**The factions.** Every faction is drawn from all three peoples; no people speaks with one voice.
- **Waystation Compact**: the station network. Trade, bounties, outfitting, law. Most of it is exactly what it looks like. A small circle inside it, the **Quiet Table**, knows the truth and sends the **Passage**, pilots who do not know where they are bound, to the stones for a steadier Count near its stations.
- **Free Reaches**: pirates and salvagers at the Edge-ward fringe who watched whole systems go dark and who refuse to touch a monolith. They raid Compact convoys because, as they see it, the Compact is feeding people to a machine.
- **Chorus of the Count**: scholars at the rare B- and E-class stars who heard the counting and have spent generations trying to read the number. They sincerely believe being copied is a gift, to be remembered forever. They hide nothing; they did not know what the number meant until Act II.
- **The Settled**: the three peoples and their cities. By Act III they become the Claimants.
- **The Patient and the Hollow**: the antagonists. The Patient can be spoken to; the Hollow cannot.

## 5. The three peoples and home worlds (placeholder names)

### 5.1 Principles
- The peoples are **alien species**, told apart by biology and by the world that shaped them, never by dress, wealth, work or any real-world culture. None is a villain and none speaks with one voice: the Compact, the Reaches and the Chorus all draw members from all three.
- Each people carries one old saying about the stars, and the three sayings are the three endings of Act IV. No saying is right.
- **Three Tier 0 home systems** within ~10 ly of each other: today's system 0 (lush home world, already forced) plus its two nearest neighbours, where we force an exotic and a toxic home world. The toxic home world is a mild variant: breathable in the lowlands, hazard only above the mist line, no ship hazard. Each system has its Waystation. New players of the three peoples meet within one warp or an hour's flight.
- **Character creation**: pick a people (sets home world, body model, starting system), name, look (colour cells, crystal pattern or spore-frond colour within that people's palette). No stat differences. Each people has one biological perk, one Tier I node pre-unlocked (not bought), a ship livery, and its own Act III anchor chain, which differs in gameplay flavour (defence, memory dive, descent), not in moral standing.
- **Every capital has the same anatomy**, built on today's planned city plains (1.1–2.1 km radius, radial or grid street plan, towers 170–340 m at the core): the **plaza** with the Anchor monolith (Recall point, centre of a 5 km safe zone); **market hall** (player market + NPC vendors); **skill master's house**; **Compact office** and quest board; **Chorus listening house**; **shipyard** with pads and the ship ladder; **salvage yard** (A Ship of Your Own); gate districts out to the wilds. Each people gets its own Blender building kit (low / mid / tall classes, 4 variants each) and NPC walkers.
- **Bodies.** Today the player is a camera and a gloved hand; other players need bodies. Use glTF skinned bipeds (three.js SkinnedMesh): one shared skeleton, three meshes, one animation set (idle, walk, run, jump, jet, swim, mine, three casts, emotes). City crowds can use the cheaper instanced vertex-shader rig the creatures already use (the biped plan, with arms on the unused wing channels 7/8).

### 5.2 Thalune: Sarelun
Sarelun stands on a shallow coastal shelf of the lush home world: sea-walls, stilt halls and canal streets that flood at high tide and drain at low, shell-ribbed roofs, and the anchor stone out in the shoals, reached by wading when the tide is out. The Thalune build the cluster's broadest hulls, shaped like the shells they grew up among. Their saying, *the tide takes and the tide returns*, is a cosmology of cycles: nothing is lost, only carried elsewhere, which is why some Thalune can hear the Feed ending without flinching. **Anchor chain, "The Tide Comes In"** (Act III, defence): the Hollow ride the tides into the shoals toward the anchor; you hold the shoals across three tides beside the city's wardens, learn from the Compact office's records why the Hollow come, and the city votes whether to wall its anchor off from the sea or leave it open. **A Ship of Your Own**: a hull beached on the tidal flats.

### 5.3 Ixari: Quavel
Quavel is grown into the stone spires of the exotic home world: halls hollowed into the spire shafts, bridges of grown crystal between the tops, spiral streets climbing to a plaza at the summit, the whole city lit at night by the world's own bioluminescence. The Ixari feel the anchor's hum in the crystal of their own spines, so Quavel's stone is the loudest in the cluster to them. Their saying, *what is heard is kept*, is a cosmology of memory: to be remembered is to persist, which is why the Hold ending speaks to them. **Anchor chain, "The Memory Dive"** (Act III, memory dive): you enter the anchor's memories and play through fragments: the Cartographers' leaving, and the pilots the stone has taken, some of them faces from Act I. The Compact office's Passage lists are found here. The city decides whether to go on listening. **A Ship of Your Own**: a listener's ship left at a monolith in the spires.

### 5.4 Ulgani: Belost
Belost is rings of grown chitin and fungal shelf over a lake that glows under the acid rain, streets sheltered by giant caps, bridges of root, and the anchor stone on a root-island at the lake's centre. The Ulgani are a mycelial people; their saying, *nothing grows alone*, is a cosmology of connection, and the Break ending, which keeps the living together whatever the cost, is its natural end. **Anchor chain, "The Root Network"** (Act III, descent): the anchor's roots run under the whole city and a Warden is already stirring beneath them; you descend through the root caverns to it, learn from the Compact office that the stirring was known and kept quiet, and the city decides whether to cut the roots. **A Ship of Your Own**: a wreck sunk in the moor outside the rings.

## 6. Where a player starts

- Account + character: pick a people (sets home world and body model), name, look.
- Cold open adrift above your home world in a dead ship (today's title shot), towed into the home Waystation.
- First hour: hangar hub, the Quartermaster hologram's first jobs (today's tutorial chain), first landing, mining, scanning, creatures, your capital city, then the Touch at the city's anchor monolith.

## 7. Death and the Recall

The network remembers you from the moment of the Touch. When your health would hit zero, the **Recall** fires: the suit's glyphs light, your body dissolves into monolith-light, a hard flash, and you wake kneeling at your home capital's anchor stone. Character, skills and inventory are kept. The ship is lost. Buy a new one at any Waystation, or if you cannot afford one, take the home-planet quest **A Ship of Your Own**: salvage and repair a wreck at a crash site outside the capital (reuses crash sites). PvP kills in lawful tiers put a bounty on the killer.

## 8. The player's story

### Prologue: Adrift (Tier 0, your home system, ~2 hours)
1. **Cold Open** (cutscene). You wake in a dead ship above your home world. Instruments dark except a faint hum. The Waystation hails and tows you in. You were found drifting; your logs are wiped; nobody knows how long you were out. The Quartermaster stakes you a basic ship on credit.
2. **First Jobs.** Today's tutorial chain as quests: land, gather, find a site, analyze a creature, return to orbit, dock, trade.
3. **The Capital.** Fly to your people's city. Markets, the Compact office, a Chorus listener, your first skill trainer. Learn the city is built around a monolith.
4. **The Touch** (cutscene). The listener sends you to the anchor stone. Two suns, a voice counting backwards, a name you almost remember. You wake with your first skill point and the Recall mark. "It remembers you now." The skill web opens.
5. **Leaving Home.** The Waystation gives you a courier run to a neighbouring Tier 1 system. Warp or fly.

### Act I: The Missing (Tiers 1–2, levels 5–20)
6. **The Courier Run.** Deliver cargo. On arrival the station is dealing with a crash on the planet below and sends you down. Ship intact, cargo intact, pilot gone. No body. No footprints past the monolith nearby.
7. **Salvage Rights.** Free Reaches scouts arrive at the same wreck. First real fight, on foot and in space. They let you live. One says: "Stay off the stones, newblood."
8. **The Settled's Trouble.** A city on a Tier 1 civilised world: creatures have turned aggressive around a monolith. Clear them (first predator mobs) and learn they are drawn to the stone. The city's harbourmaster hands you a registry: forty pilots missing this year, every one near a monolith.
9. **The Beacon Net.** Activate three signal beacons across a world to triangulate a distress call. It leads to a Hollow drifter: a ship with no one aboard still flying its course (cutscene: the seat is empty, the stick moves).
10. **The Outpost Logs.** Decrypt logs at several abandoned outposts. They record a Compact office paying for "passage" at monolith sites, and the outposts emptied in a hurry. Compact reputation rises; doubt seeds.
11. **Raid on the Waystation** (instanced for you and your party; the repeatable station-raid event is the shared version). A pirate wing hits the station. Defend it, follow the wing to the Scrapyard, board it, capture its captain. She tells you where the pilots go: "ask the Chorus what the number means."
12. **The Sanctuary** (cutscene). Travel to a B-class star, the Chorus's home. The galaxy map lights every monolith; the Count is laid over the cluster and it is falling faster than it used to. The Chorus admit the monoliths copy travellers and call it a gift.

### Act II: The Count (Tiers 2–3, levels 20–35)
13. **Reading the Number.** To read the Count, the Chorus need three monolith memories from hostile worlds: an inferno world, a corrosive world, an irradiated world. The first dungeon worlds: hazards, sentinels, crystal horrors. Each memory is a cutscene fragment of the Cartographers' past (the mapping, the discovery, the sealing).
14. **The Burned Road.** A Reaches contact offers passage to the fringe for a job: intercept a Compact convoy carrying the Passage, pilots bound for the stones who do not know it. Intercept it, or warn the Compact. First faction fork; reputation shifts, not final.
15. **The Dark Systems.** Fly to a system The Edge has nearly reached. The sky is half wall. The Waystation is dead, the cities empty, Hollow everywhere. Find the Reaches' survivors from the dark systems (cutscene: their leader shows you the stars that are no longer there).
16. **The Dreadnought.** Fight beside the Reaches' flagship against a Hollow husk swarm. Earn their standing. They teach what the Chorus will not: the Void branch of the skill web unlocks.
17. **What the Number Says** (cutscene). Back at the sanctuary with the three memories: the Count is not time until The Edge arrives. It is time until the sleepers wake. The Chorus split: some want to wake the Patient early and be saved by them, some want to stop the Count.
18. **Descent** (raid). The crushing world: pressure hazard all the way down, the murk, the thing that moves in it. Reach the sleeping Cartographer. It speaks through the stones and explains the plan plainly: collapse the cluster into a seed, push it through The Edge, everyone recorded goes with it. It thanks you for your copy.

### Act III: Claimants (Tiers 3–4, levels 35–45)
19. **The Quiet Table.** A small circle inside the Compact, drawn from all three peoples, has known for generations and sends the Passage to the stones for a steadier Count near its stations. Expose them or join them. **The faction choice**: Compact (keep the Count running, decide who feeds it) or Reaches (break the Count, risk The Edge). PvP factions form here.
20. **The Settled Rise.** Rally the three capitals: three city chains, your own home city's chain longest. The peoples name themselves the Claimants: "the stars belong to no one, so no one gets to spend us."
21. **The Wardens.** The Patient wake their guardians: you face your first Warden in an instanced fight scaled to your party, while in the shared world Wardens rise on the Tier 4 worlds and the Hollow surge inward as events.
22. **The Anchor Stones.** Learn that the three capitals stand on the three anchors of the Count, and that the network can be reached through them. Each people's anchor chapter plays out in its own city, in sight of the players who live there.

### Act IV: The Edge (Tier 5, levels 45–50)
23. **The Long Flight.** No warp works near the wall. The thirty-minute interstellar cruise becomes a set piece: Hollow tides, a dying sky, the Compact and Reaches fleets flying the same road for different reasons.
24. **At the Wall** (cutscene). The Edge fills the sky. The truth: it is not the universe ending. It is the boundary of the last seed. The Cartographers have done this before. The counting backwards is how many times.
25. **The Choice.** Three ways to act at the wall:
   - **Feed**: let the Count finish. Your copy goes with the seed into a new universe. The living stay behind. The Patient's way.
   - **Break**: shatter the anchors. The Count stops, The Edge speeds up, and the cluster is free to fight for whatever time it has. The Reaches' way.
   - **Hold**: keep the Count running, but by consent: volunteers only, no Passage. The long middle. The Compact's way, reformed.
   Your choice sets your personal epilogue and alignment. Server-wide, all choices feed the **Count meter**, which drives the seasons.
26. **Epilogue and endgame.** Hollow hives, Edge expeditions, Warden rotations, anchor defence (PvP), and seasonal Count cycles where the meter's state changes which systems are Edge-ward, which stations are dark, and where the Hollow tides run.

**Cutscenes in-engine** (camera glides, dips, banners already exist; add a dialogue bar): Cold Open, the Touch, the Hollow drifter, the Sanctuary map, three Cartographer memories, the survivors' sky, the Descent, the Wall, three endings.

## 9. The skill web

### 9.1 Rules

**No classes.** Every character has the same body of possible skills: one web of seven branches. A node must be **unlocked** (by the world) and then **bought** (with a skill point). Unlocks come from where you went and who you talked to, so two players at the same level know different things.

**Points.** 1 per level (50) + 1 per main-story chapter (26) + a handful from faction chains ≈ 80 points. Nodes cost 1 point, capstones 3, conjunctions 2. The web holds 7 × 20 + 6 conjunctions = 146 nodes, ~160 points to own everything, so a finished build is about half the web.

**Tiers.** Tier I nodes from level 1, Tier II from level 15, Tier III from level 30, capstones from level 45 and a story chapter. Within a branch each node needs one node of the tier below (any). Conjunctions need Tier II in both branches.

**Slots.** 6 ground actives (keys 1–6) and 4 ship actives (keys 1–4 while flying). The multitool beam stays on LMB as the base tool (mining); ship lasers stay on LMB as the base gun. Passives are always on. Swap slots anywhere out of combat.

**Unlocks.**
- **Memory fragments.** Every monolith you touch grants one fragment keyed to its world's branch: Gravitic on barren, dead and frozen worlds; Thermal on volcanic, inferno and desert; Resonance on exotic, storm and toxic; Symbiotic on lush worlds (and any world where you have analyzed every species); Void on irradiated, corrosive and crushing worlds (usable only with Free Reaches standing). Tier I nodes unlock with 1 fragment of the branch, Tier II with 3, Tier III with 6.
- **Trainers.** Chorus listeners (Resonance Tier II–III, respec), city masters (Symbiotic, Exo-suit), Waystation outfitters (Pilot, Thermal ship forms), Free Reaches quartermasters (all Void). Trainers need faction standing.
- **Story.** Capstones unlock at specific chapters: Gravitic and Thermal at ch. 13 (the three memories), Resonance at ch. 17, Symbiotic at ch. 20, Void at ch. 16, Pilot and Exo-suit at ch. 23.
- **Conjunctions** unlock by discovery (see 9.9).

**Heat (ground).** 0–100. Abilities add heat in chunks; the beam adds 32/s (today's rate). Cooling 20/s idle, 8/s while channelling. At 100 you overheat: no abilities until heat falls below 25 (today's rule). World modifiers: frozen and cryo worlds +50% cooling; desert, volcanic and inferno worlds in daylight −40%; underwater +100%; night +10%. Passives raise capacity or cooling; Vent and Meltdown spend heat on purpose.

**Charge (ship).** 0–100, today's pulse fuel. Ship actives spend it. Regenerates 2/s in sunlight inside the habitable band, nothing in deep space, instant paid refill at any Waystation (today's Pulse Fuel item). The pulse drive still burns 0.35/s near bodies.

**Respec.** Any Chorus listener, 500 units per point refunded, Void nodes only at a Reaches quartermaster.

## 10. Ships and weapons

### 10.1 Principles
- A ship is a **hull** plus **modules**. The hull sets performance, health, signature, cargo and slots; **weapons** are modules fitted to hardpoints; the hull's **ability slots** run the ship forms of your skill web. Your ship build follows your ground build.
- Ships are **lost on death**, so the ship ladder is the economy's real sink. Suit inventory stays with the character; the ship's **cargo pods drop where it died** as a lootable pod (anyone can take it; the Exo-suit node Recall Lattice protects it for you 10 min).
- **Stealth is a number.** Each hull has a **signature** (1.0 = today's ship). Detection distance = detector's sensor range × your signature. It sets when pirates roll a raid on you, when the Hollow turn toward you, and how far away other players' HUD markers pick you up. Belts, rings and nebulae halve it; boosting or pulsing doubles it; Veil, Cloak and Silent Running stack on top.
- Today's values as the baseline: speed 600 space / 240 atmosphere, boost ×2.6, hull 100, shield 100, charge 100, two pulse lasers to 1500 m.

## 12. Mobs

### 12.1 Combat model (what mobs need from the engine)
- **Health and level.** Every mob has a level (1–50) and a threat rating (1–5 stars, shown after analysis). Health = base × (1 + 0.12 × level); damage likewise. Players have 100 health today; Second Skin raises it to 150.
- **Hits.** Ground abilities resolve as rays, cones or spheres against mob hit radii (the fauna picker already does sphere picks along the aim ray). Ship weapons use the existing hitscan against ship/asteroid spheres.
- **Aggro.** Each mob has a sense radius (sight) and a mind radius (the Hollow's). Threat goes to whoever hurt it most recently, modified by Veil (−), Harmonic (reset), Bond companions (+taunt).
- **Resistances by branch.** Crystal creatures resist Thermal, break to Shatter. The Hollow ignore Symbiotic poison except Rot, and flee Nullsong. Sentinels and Wardens shrug Gravitic pulls (they are heavy) and are weak to Dissonance. Pirates are people: no resistances, they use the skill web too.
- **Death and drops.** Units, resources, **Count residue** (crafting material from stone-touched and Hollow), ship parts (space), codex entries. Fragments come only from monoliths, never from kills.
- **Animation.** Fauna attacks are new states in the existing vertex-shader rig (lunge, charge, bite, dive) driven by gait and a per-instance attack phase. Hollow ships are existing hulls with a husked material (dark, glyph-lit seams, missing panels). Sentinels, Wardens and crews are new Blender models; crews use the skinned biped rig.

### 12.3 Stone-touched (the Count's reach)
On worlds with many fed stones (Tier 3+, and any world during a Hollow tide), herds within 500 m of a monolith turn **stone-touched**: glyph-lit hides (the creature rig's glow channel), crystal growths, no fear, drawn to the stone and to anything with a mind. They attack players, pirates and docile herds alike. A stone-touched herd mother leads them (see bosses). Killing them drops Count residue; Harmonic does not calm them, Veil hides you from them, Nullsong breaks the stone's hold for 30 s and they scatter.

### 12.4 Lifeless-world horrors
The hostile archetypes get their own fauna, always hostile, immune to their world's hazard:
- **Dead worlds**: shamblers, the petrified husks of old herds that stand still until approached (crystal rig, slow, hit hard).
- **Irradiated**: crystal stalkers, glowing, resist Thermal, shatter to Resonance; radiation static on your HUD grows as they close.
- **Corrosive**: acid swimmers that lunge from the acid seas and rain pools; rot armour.
- **Crushing**: murk-things, seen only as movement in the violet, that latch on and crush (pressure damage on foot).
- **Inferno**: cinder wings, flyers that ride the thermals and drop embers; the only fauna in the fire.

## 14. Quests

### 14.1 The quest engine (grows out of Objectives.js)
Today's objective chain is a list of steps, each with a `text(game)` and a `done(game)` predicate, one active index saved as a number, and a toast when the index moves. That is the seed. Generalise it:
- **Quest definition is data, not code**: id, title, category, giver (NPC, terminal, monolith, board), prerequisites (level, chapter, faction rank, flags, people), steps, rewards, flags set on completion, repeat rule (once, cooldown, daily), and whether each step is **open-world** or **instanced**.
- **Step types**: travel (reach a body, site or point within a radius), interact (terminal, monolith, NPC), collect (resource × n), mine (deposit kind × n), analyze (n species, or one named species), scan (reveal n sites), kill (mob tag × n), defend (hold a point for t seconds against waves), escort (an NPC ship from A to B), deliver (item to NPC), survive (t seconds below a hazard ceiling), touch (a monolith), dialogue (choices set flags and rep), cutscene (trigger), choice (fork to different next steps).
- **Presentation**: each step emits HUD markers (the marker system already draws poi, planet, station, enemy, resource and ship kinds; add quest), the objective line under the location panel (exists), a journal, and a **dialogue bar** (speaker name, a bust of the people's model, text, 2–4 choices).
- **Instancing**: a step flagged instanced spawns its actors (NPCs, bosses, props) in a private layer at the shared location; party members on the same step share the layer; everyone else sees the ordinary world there. Cutscenes are always instanced.
- **Authority**: the server owns quest state and evaluates predicates from world events (kill, pickup, interact, enter radius); the client predicts for the UI. Character state holds a map of quest id → step index and counters, replacing today's single objective index.

### 14.3 Boards: generated from the live world
Each board offers 3–5 jobs, one of each kind, refreshed every 30 minutes, scaled to the system's tier:
- **Bounty**: a pirate wing or predator pack that actually exists in the system right now (the raid timer and the den planner are the sources). Reward by tier.
- **Delivery**: n units of a resource this station is short of (the dynamic economy's demand) to a named station or city. Reward = price gap + fee.
- **Survey**: analyze n species on a planet with unanalysed species; or mine n deposits of a kind.
- **Salvage**: visit n unvisited crash sites or outposts within the system (Structures already tracks visited sites).
- **Escort**: ride with the next departing traffic ship (Traffic already schedules arrivals and departures) to its destination; pirates roll a raid on the way.
- **Patrol**: fly through three points: the belt, a moon, the outer planet; reveals drifters and lurkers.
City boards add civic jobs: clear predators from the gate districts, gather sodium plants for the apothecary, repair the beacons on the city's world, a census of the world's species.

## 15. Economy

### 15.1 Today, and the shape of the change
Today: nine resources at flat prices sold at any station, a shop of three consumables and six upgrades, generous one-shot payouts from sites and discoveries, and two death taxes (20% of units for a lost hull, 15% for a tow). The change: resources tiered by where they come from, prices that move per station, buying as well as selling, a regional player market, crafting as the main use for raw materials, and the ship ladder as the sink that replaces the death taxes.

### 15.2 Currencies
- **Units**: the only money. Earned from everything, spent on ships, modules, ammo, services and the market.
- **Count residue**: a material, not money, from stone-touched herds and the Hollow. Crafts glyph cosmetics and the Husk hull's parts. Tradeable.
- **Fragments**: never tradeable. Monoliths and surveys only (see the skill web).
- **Reputation**: per faction, never tradeable.

### 15.4 Prices move
Every station and capital keeps its own price per resource:
`price = base × tier factor × local supply factor × sell-pressure factor × event factor`, clamped to 0.5–2.0 × base.
- **Tier factor**: a resource far from where it is common costs more (carbon at a barren-system station, murkpearl anywhere but Tier 4).
- **Local supply**: what this system's planets and belt actually hold (the deposit kinds the generator picked, the archetypes present). A lush system is a carbon glut; a system with a storm world sells stormquartz cheap.
- **Sell pressure**: every player sale pushes the local price down a little; it recovers over hours. Dumping ten thousand ferrite at one station halves its price there; the next station is untouched. That makes hauling worth doing.
- **Events**: a tide raises ammo and ship prices in the system for a day; a lost convoy raises the goods it carried; a season at low Count raises everything Edge-ward.
- **Spread**: stations buy at the price and sell at 1.25 × price. Capitals have the tightest spreads; the Scrapyard the widest.
Shortages (a resource the station holds too little of) are what the board's delivery jobs are generated from, so quests and prices are the same system.

### 15.5 The player market
- **Regional order books** per system, at the Waystation and the capital: buy and sell orders for resources, modules, ammo, recipes, liveries and glyph cosmetics. Nothing teleports between systems; a Longhaul full of cheap carbon flown to a barren system is the trade game.
- **Fees**: 5% on a filled order, halved at Neighbour city rank; listings expire after seven days.
- **The Scrapyard market**: the Reaches' black market. Void recipes, stolen convoy cargo, hulls with no questions asked, at wide spreads. Reaches rank gates the good stock.
- **No trading of**: fragments, skill points, reputation, story items. Count residue and Husk alloy trade freely.

### 15.6 Faucets and sinks

Other sinks: modules and ammo, warp cells, pulse refills, repairs, respecs (500 units a point), market fees, liveries, and the glyph cosmetics. The two death taxes go away; losing the ship is the tax.
Today's site payouts (600–1,600 units from an outpost or monolith at Tier 0) are cut to a third at Tier 0 and scale with tier, so the first ship upgrade is a goal rather than a given.

New players can never be locked out: the stake ship is always one quest away at home.

## 16. The Count meter and seasons

### 16.1 What the meter is
One server-wide number, the **Count**: the network's remaining charge, 0–1,000 **measures**. It burns down on its own, a measure every few hours, and moves with what players do. It is the thing the whole story is about, so the world should visibly change with it.

### 16.2 What moves it: lean, not volume
The meter must work the same with ten players as with ten thousand, so it never adds player actions up. It reads their **lean**.
- Every counted action is tagged Feed or Break (Hold counts as a third of a Feed). Feed actions: a Passage convoy escorted to its destination, a Feed or Hold choice at the wall, an anchor held by Feed or Hold at the week's end. Break actions: a Passage convoy raided and its pilots freed, a Break choice at the wall, an anchor held by Break, a Hive Heart shattered, a Warden killed. Every monolith touched by anyone is a tiny Feed, because everyone is copied.
- Each day the server computes the **lean**: Feed actions as a share of all counted actions that day, one vote per account per action type so nobody grinds it. A day with no actions has no lean.
- The meter drifts toward a **target** set by the lean: 850 at all-Feed, 150 at all-Break, 500 at an even split, at a fixed speed of up to 25 measures a day. Ten players who all raid convoys lean the world exactly as hard as ten thousand.
- Under that, the Count **burns 5 measures a day on its own**. A world nobody tends slides slowly toward Broken and the Edge advances. That is the story's pressure, and it means the season has a shape even on a quiet server: the Edge creeps, and whoever is playing decides whether to fight it or welcome it.
- The underdog side (the one the lean is against) earns bonus rep while the meter sits more than 200 from the middle, so a thin population does not lock into one ending.

### 16.3 What the meter does to the world
| Count | The Edge | Tiers | Hollow | Passage | Stones | Economy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| High (700+) | recedes: the Tier 5 band shrinks by a few systems; dark stations relight | Edge-ward systems step down a tier | tides rare | Compact runs more Passage convoys (more flashpoints) | stone-touched rare; Wardens dormant | trade cheap, Count residue scarce |
| Middle (300–700) | baseline | as designed | hourly at Tier 5 | normal | normal | baseline |
| Low (under 300) | advances: Edge-ward systems flip to Tier 5; their Waystations go dark and become Hive raids | the next shell steps up a tier | tides reach Tier 3, every half hour at Tier 5 | no Passage; the Compact pulls back | stone-touched spread to Tier 2; Wardens rise weekly | Edge-ward prices double, Count residue and Void fragments common |
Technically this is cheap: the Edge's position (`edgeX`) and each system's tier become functions of the meter, the deterministic world does the rest, and a "dark" station is the Hive variant of the station build. The galaxy map shows the Edge where it currently is.

### 16.4 Seasons and the Reckoning
A season is eight weeks by default, four while the active population is small (the server picks at season start from the last month's daily actives). The meter starts a season at 500. In the final week the **Reckoning** opens at the wall: a Tier 5 event where NPC fleets of the three philosophies fight the tides and each other whether or not any player comes, scaled to whoever does. Every player present casts their Act IV choice again, counted as that day's lean; players who are not there are still counted through the season's accumulated lean, so the live event is a bonus, never a requirement. The bucket the meter ends in decides the server's ending for the season:
- **Fed** (700+): the Patient's way. The Edge recedes for next season's opening map; the Count's cost shows as more missing pilots and more Hollow drifters mid-season.
- **Broken** (under 300): the Reaches' way. The Edge advances for next season; two or three systems start dark; the Hollow are everywhere and the Void is strong; the living are free.
- **Held** (300–700): the long middle. Baseline map; the Passage convoys stop and only volunteers feed the Count; the hardest state to keep, because it needs both sides restrained.
The winning philosophy's players get the season's title and glyph; everyone gets a season chronicle (what the server did, who named what). Your personal Act IV ending is still yours; the Reckoning is what the world remembers.

### 16.5 Where players see it
The Chorus sanctuaries show the Count on a great glyph wall for anyone. The Void node Edge Sense puts it on your HUD. The galaxy map shows the Edge's position and which systems are dark. Banners announce tides, risings and the Reckoning. A weekly server message tells the number, and the season chronicle names each account's measures, so on a small server one player can read exactly what they did to the world.

### 16.6 One online world, at five players and at five thousand
There is no offline mode and no fallback. The mechanics themselves must hold at both extremes, so every shared system is designed against two tests: **"three people are online"** and **"three thousand are in the home system."** Where the first draft failed one of them, the mechanic is changed below, not patched.

| System | Fails at three | Fails at three thousand | What passes both |
|---|---|---|---|

## 17. MMO architecture (sketch)

Online only, from the first build. An authoritative server owns everything that is shared; the client owns rendering, the deterministic world, and prediction.
- **World from seeds.** The universe is regenerated client-side from the shared seeds exactly as today, so the server never streams terrain, flora or sites. It streams only dynamic state: players, mobs, quest state, boss state, prices, discoveries, control bars and the Count.
- **Shards and zones.** One world shard per region until it would overflow, merging before splitting. Inside a shard, one zone process per star system, with planet surfaces sub-zoned by region as players spread. Zones with nobody in them sleep; NPC baselines (convoys, tides, burn) are simulated coarsely by a world service while they sleep and resumed in detail when a player arrives.
- **Phases are first class.** Story chapters, hangar bays, event instances and raid instances are all phases: a private layer at a shared location with a cap (1 party, 24 in a bay, 12 in an event). The phase manager opens and closes them by presence, which is what lets the same place serve three players or three thousand.
- **Persistent objectives.** Boss and objective state (a Warden's shell, a Hive Heart's defences, an anchor's control bar) lives on the server with weekly resets, so progress accumulates across sessions and players.
- **Meter and seasons.** A small world service computes the daily lean, drifts the Count, moves the Edge, flips tiers and dark stations, schedules drift content and the Reckoning.
- **Economy service.** Per-station price state, order books, board generation and sell-pressure decay.
- **Social.** Shard-wide chat, parties, fleets (guilds), the call-for-aid beacon and matchmaking into open phases.
- **Transport and persistence.** WebSocket (WebTransport later) with client prediction for movement and server reconciliation; Postgres for accounts, characters, quest and boss state, discoveries and the chronicle; Redis for live zone state.

## 18. Roadmap

Every phase ships as the online game, playable with a handful of players from the first one.
- **Phase 1 (online core + RPG layer)**: accounts, the server with one shard and system zones, phases, player bodies for the three peoples, chat and parties; XP and levels, the skill web, hostile fauna and pirates, the quest engine, Prologue + Act I, the three capitals rendered, Recall, the ship ladder, dialogue bar and cutscene tooling, NPC convoys and station boards. - **Phase 2 (the living world)**: PvP with safe zones, patrols and bounties; factions and rep; moving prices, crafting and the player market; world events scaled to presence; the Count meter with lean and burn; Acts II and III; the Scrapyard, Sentinel Primes, the Descent; anchor control bars.
- **Phase 3 (seasons and scale)**: raids with persistent progress (Wardens, Hive Heart), Act IV and the Reckoning, seasons and drift content, hangar bays and capital phasing for thousands, shard merging, the chronicle.

## 19. Art and asset production (Blender)

### 19.1 The rule
Every model in the game is authored by hand in Blender, to one high bar. The demo's approach, Python scripts that generate geometry in headless Blender, was right for a demo and is wrong for the game: it caps quality at what a script can describe. From here, scripts export and bake; they do not model.
- **Source of truth**: `.blend` files in the repository (Git LFS), one per asset family, with named collections per variant and LOD.
- **Export**: one headless Blender script per family (replacing the `build_*.py` generators) that validates naming, bakes textures, generates LODs where none were hand-made, and writes `.glb` files to `public/models`. CI runs it; a failed validation fails the build.
- **Procedural only where it must be**: terrain, flora scatter placement, gas giants, atmosphere and the sky stay procedural. Everything they place or dress is hand-made.
- **No fallbacks**: the demo's procedural stand-ins (box outposts, primitive creatures, the box station) are deleted. A missing asset is a build error, not a downgrade.

### 19.2 The quality bar
| Area | Target |
|---|---|
| Geometry | hero assets (player bodies, hulls, Wardens, the Patient, each capital's landmarks) 60–150k triangles at LOD0; creatures 15–40k; props 2–10k. Four LODs per asset, LOD3 under 5% of LOD0. Flora keeps the impostor hand-over beyond 400 m. |
| Materials | full PBR: base colour, roughness, metallic, normal, ambient occlusion, emissive. 4k textures on heroes, 2k on creatures and buildings, 1k on props, atlases per kit. Hand-painted wear and detail; the demo's panel-detail shader survives only as a micro-detail layer under real texturing. |
| Rigging | one shared humanoid skeleton for the three peoples, crews and city NPCs, with facial blendshapes for the dialogue bar. Creatures keep the instanced vertex-shader rig for crowds, with hand-weighted limbs and hand-authored attack poses; hero creatures and bosses are fully skinned. Hulls articulate gear, canopy, hardpoint mounts and thruster nozzles, and carry damage and husked states. |
| Animation | hand-keyed in Blender: locomotion sets, jet and swim, mining, casts per branch, emotes, creature attacks, boss phases. Cutscene camera paths are authored in Blender and exported as curves the engine's camera-glide system follows. |
| Consistency | one art bible: the three peoples' design languages (shell, crystal, lattice), the Cartographer glyph alphabet, the Hollow's husked treatment, Compact and Reaches liveries, a shared material library. A 1 m grid and a 1.85 m biped are the scale references in every file. |
| Review | every asset is checked in-engine under three lighting cases (noon on a lush world, night on an exotic world, open space) before it merges, with a turntable render attached to the pull request. |

### 19.4 Conventions the engine reads
Keep what the engine already understands so content stays simple: material names on hulls (Hull, Accent, Trim, EngineGlow, Nav*, Glass) for liveries and recolours; `COL_` meshes, `Interact`, `Light_`, `Blink` and `Spin` markers on structures; `_limb`, `_pivot` and `_glow` attributes on creatures; colour-region masks on flora. Add: LOD collections, hardpoint and bay empties, skeleton and blendshape naming, cutscene camera curves.

## 20. From demo to game: keep, rework, remove

The demo proved the engine and the feel. The design does not owe it anything else.

### 20.1 Keep: the foundation
Floating origin and frames of reference. Cube-sphere terrain LOD in workers. The atmosphere, cloud, ocean and sky passes. The universe at true scale with deterministic generation, the 70-system cluster, planet archetypes, hazards, weather and day/night. The flight model, landing, docking autopilot, pulse drive and interstellar cruise. The first-person walker with jetpack and swimming. The heat meter and scanner pulse as the seed of abilities. The site planner with its levelled pads, and the species generator and instanced creature rig as systems (their current models go). The marker, HUD, banner and toast systems. The galaxy and system maps. The camera-glide tooling. Procedural audio. The dev tools.

### 20.2 Rework: the system stays, the content is replaced
| Demo | Game |
|---|---|
| one script-built box station: 4 pads, 2 kiosks, a decorative hologram | a modular Blender Waystation with bays, a full hub, the Quartermaster as an NPC |
| cities planned but never rendered | three capitals from people-specific kits, plus the civilised worlds' cities |
| a 7-step objective chain | the quest engine and 26 chapters |
| 9 flat-priced resources, a sell-only terminal, a 9-item shop | 19 tiered resources, moving prices, buying and selling, the player market, fabricators, the ship ladder, the skill web |
| one ship model reused for traffic, pirates and wrecks | the 13-hull roster with liveries and husked variants |
| random-timer pirate raids in recoloured player ships | faction AI: scouts, wings, raiders, convoys, the Scrapyard |
| passive fauna analysed for units | temperaments, predators, stone-touched herds, horrors, Bond; analysis feeds the codex and the web |
| one-shot site payouts | sites as quest givers, dens, vaults, glyph puzzles |
| five lore lines | the backstory, dialogue, cutscenes and mysteries |
| suit failure and a ship rebuilt for 20% of units | the Recall; the ship is lost; A Ship of Your Own |
| a gloved hand and one multitool | per-people first-person arms and tools |
| a localStorage save and a title screen | accounts, character creation, server-held state; the title shot becomes the Cold Open |
| discovery banners paying units | first-finder naming, surveys, the codex, XP |

### 20.3 Remove
The procedural fallback builders (`buildOutpost`, `buildMonolith`, `buildBeacon`, `buildCrash`, `buildQuadruped`, `buildFlyer`, and the procedural tree builders wherever the library covers them). The box-geometry station build. The flat `RESOURCES` price table, the sell-only trade panel and the `SHOP` list (their items move into the ship ladder, fabricators and the skill web). The two death taxes. The random raid timer. The "THE STARS ARE YOURS" training banner. The localStorage save. The `build_*.py` geometry generators, replaced by export scripts. The dev teleporter and scenario hashes stay as development tools only.
